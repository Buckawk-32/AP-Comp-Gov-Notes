# Impact of Industriallization & Economic Development

## Learning Objectives
1. Explain how rapid industrialization and economic development have produced radical changes in governmental policies

## Industrialization
- -> process by which a country or region transforms its economy from being primarily agricultural to one based on manufacturing and industry
    - Features:
        - Shift from manual labor to mechanized production
        - Urbanization
        - Transportation & communication
        - Growth of capitalism & *wage labor*

- Impact of Industrialization
    1. Economic Growth -> Higher production, increased trade, and job creation
    2. Techonoligcal Advancements -> Incentions like the steam engine, electricity, and automation
    3. Social & Enviromental Challenges -> Exploitation of workers, pollution, wealth inequality

- Has come about in phases or waves


## 1st Wave of Industrialization
- 1750 ~ 1850
    - Started in Britian, caught on in limited ways in France, Germany, & the US starting in the 1830s
        - *Why Britian?* 
            - Agrarian surplus, natural resources (coal, iron), capital access 

    - Transistion from agranrian to industrial
    - Key Inventions:
        - Steam engine
        - Mechanized textiles
        - Iron production


## 2nd Wave of Industrialization
- 1850 ~ 1950
    - Western Europe, North America, Japan
    - Expansion of heavy industry: steel, chemicals, oils, electricity 
    - From factories to *mass production* & *assembly lines*
        - Henry Ford's Model T
    - Coroporate monopolies
        - Rockefeller, Carnegie

    - Economic impact: Rise of global trade, imperialism by resource demand
    - Coincided with the rise of nationalism


## 3rd Wave of Industrialization
- 1950 ~ 1990s
    - Also called the Digital Revolution
    - Shift from heavy industry to technology & services
        - Rise of computers, automation, robotics, telecommunications
    - Globalization & free-market capitalism (ex. rise of multinational corporations)

    - Economic Impact: Expansion of international trade, outsourcing & supply chains, rise of Silicon Valley


## 4th Wave of Industrialization
- 2000s ~ Present
    - Key Features:
        - AI, robotics, big data, blockchain, IoT (Internet of Things)
        - Automation replacing traditional manufacturing (Ex. self-driving cars, smart factories)
        - Rise of difital platforms (Amazon, Uber, Cloud Computing)

    - Economic Impact: Job displacement vs. new industries, devates over tech regulation & privacy


## Consequences of Industrialization
- Exploitation of workers & poor working conditions
- Urban crowding & poor living conditions
- Increased wealth inequality & class struggle
- Imperialism
- Global conflicts & mechanized warfare
- Job losses due to automation
- Enviromental destruction


## Enviromental Damage
- Fossil fuels
- Affects all countries:
    - UK: Great Smog of London killed 12,000 people in 1952
    - Russia: Chernobyl

- Priority = economic growth
- But then it becomes too big to ignore
    - Demand from the people:
        - Healthcare needed by affected citizens
        - Lobbying & citizen protests
        - Pressure from international community


## Responses to Industrialization:
### Enviromental Protection:
- Solutions include:
    1. Regulating factories
        - UK = leader in offshore wind farms
    2. Regulating cars
        - Common in Mexican cities
    3. Preventing pollution from the public
        - China's drive for more efficient HSR

### More Economic Liberalization:
- Trade liberalization affects:
    - the growth of foreign & domestic businesses
    - Amount of FDI
    - & the quality of the enviroment

- Lower tarrifs =>  lower consumer costs at the expense of domestic industry

### Austerity:
- Budget deficits -> austerity
    - Tax increases and/or budget cuts
        - Often of social policies
