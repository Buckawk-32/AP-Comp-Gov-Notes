# Impact of Social Movements and Political Groups 

## Learning Objectives:
1. Explain how social movements and interest groups affect social and political change

## Social Movements
- -> Large groups of people pushing collectively for singificant political or social change
    - Sustained *grassroots* (-> bottom-up; community level) organization
    - Large and informal at the beginning

- Social movements are not focused on a single policy issue or specific interest but are, instead, focused on a sweeping change to all aspects of life


## Interest Groups
- -> explicitly orgainsed to represent and advocate for a specific interest or policy issue
    - Large and formal
    - Often top-down
    - People pay dues to be members
    - Key Roles:
        - Advocacy and *lobbying* of government
        - Represent members on behalf of citizens
        - Organize political demonstrations
        - Engage in negotiation and compromise with organizations and lawmakers

    - Examples:
        - Mothers Against Drunk Drivers
        - National Rifle Association


## Iranian Social Movements
- *Green Movement*
    - Protested the corruption form the 2009 Presidential election
    - Goal: Force the government into more transparent and fair elections
    - Diverse coalition
        - Activists, studnts, intellectuals, other politicians

    - Government cracked down hard
        - But symbolized a significant shift and division in Iran's politics

    - Result: 
        - Guardian Council allowed for a recount of 10% of the votes but reported that they found no irregularities and dimissed all elections

- *One Million Signatures*
    - Campaign for women's rights
        - Marital and family law, access to education, protection against violence

- *May Day*
    - Higher wages and better worker benefits
    - International movement


## Mexico Social Movements
- *Zapatista National Liberation Army (Zapatistas)*
    - 1994 -> Uprising in Chaipas
    - In response to the *NAFTA* agreement and its imapcts on indigenous peoples
    - Started as an armed insurgency
        - Government cracked down hard
    - Shifted to grassroots organizing

    - Calls for work, land, housing, food, health, education, independence, liberty, democaracy, justice, and peace for indigenous people
    - Indigenous Rights Bill of 2001
        - Preferential use of natural resources like wood and water on their territories
        

## Nigeria Social Movements
- 2 primary movements:
    1. *Movement for the Emancipation of the Niger Delta (MEND)*
        - Armed but disorganized
        - Sabotage, pipeline vandalism

    2. *Movement for the Survival of the Ogoni People (MOSOP)*
        - Umbrella organization of 11 different organizations fighting for social, economic, and enviromental justice

    - Both advocate for the rights of ethnic minorities in oil-rich regions

## Grassroots Movements: Pros & Cons
- Pros
    - Difficult to suppress
        - Loosely organized
        - Bottom-Up
        - No clear leadership that can be taken out
        - Anonymity

- Cons
    - No defined hierarchy of leadership
    - Hard to bring about real change
        - Ex. MEND
            - Splintered into 2 different groups that want 2 different things
            - Government cannot really take action
