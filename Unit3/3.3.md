# Political Ideologies

## What is Political Ideology?
- -> is a set of values and beliefs about the goals of government, public policy, or politics


## 1. Individualism
- -> belief in individual civil liberities and freedom over governmental restrictions
- Core Features
    - Prioritizes personal autonomy and self-determination
    - Advocates limited government intervention in individal rights and freedom

- Philosophical Origins
    - Enlightenment Thought (late 1700s in France, UK, and USA)
        - Ex. John Locke, Thomas Jefferson
        - Reason, equality, and liberty (Ex. John Locke's philosophy)
    - Classical liberalism, advocating for minimal state interference and protection of natural rights

- Examples:
    - USA
    - UK (somewhat)


## 2. Neoliberalism
- -> Belief in limited government intervention in the economy and society, emphasizing privatization, free trade, deregulation, and the elimination of state subsidies
- Core Features
    - Advocates for a free-market economy to promote efficieny and growth
    - Reduces the role of state in economic affairs
    - Promotes global trade liberalization and competitive markets

- Historical Context
    - Became dominant in the 1980s and 1990s with Reagen & Thatcher

- Philosophical Origins
    - Influenced by classical liberalism and economic theories of *Adam Smith* and *Friedrich Hayek*
    - Revitalized by the Chicago School of Economics in the mid-20th Century
        - Market efficiency and indivudal entrepreneurial freedom

- Examples:
    - Nigeria's Structural Adjustment Program (SAP) in the 1980s
        - Reduce state intervention and prioritize economic growth during a finanical crisis


## 3. Communism
- -> Belief in the abolition of private property with near-total governmental control of the economy
- Core Features
    - Collective ownership and the redistribution of resources
    - Classless society achieved through revolution
    - Central planning to meet the needs of all citizens

- Philosophical Origins
    - Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels
        - *Communist Manifesto*: historical materialism and class conflict
    - Vladimir Lenin included a vanguard party leading the revolution and maintaining state control

- Examples:
    - CCP -> follows a Marxist-Leninist ideology
        - Dictatorship of the proletariat, class struggle, and dialectical materialism


## 4. Socialism
- -> Belief in the reduction of income disparities and the nationalization of major private industries
- Core Features:
    - Focuses on redistributing wealth to achieve greater economic equality
    - State ownership of key industries and resources
    - Social welfare programs to meet citizens' needs

- Philosophical Origins
    - Marx & Engels
    - Utopian socialists like Henri de Saint-Simon and Charles Fourier
        - Avocating cooperative ownership and social reform

- Examples:
    - Bolshevik Revolution of 1917 established a socialist state in Russia
    - From 1917 to the Soviet Union's collapse in 1991, the government implemented socialist strategies, including central planning and state ownership of oil enterprises


## 5. Fascism
- -> Extreme nationalist ideology that favors authoritarian rule and prioritizes the rights of the ethnic majority over ethnic minorities and political opposition
- Core Features:
    - Strong centralized authority with suppression of dissent
    - Promotes milliarism and the glorification of the state
    - Rejects liberal democracy and indivdual rights in favor of collective national interests

- Philosophical Origins
    - Early 20th-century political movements, particularly Benito Mussolini's ideology in Italy
    - Draws from ultranationalist and anti-democratic princples
        - Rejecting socialism and liberalism as threats to national unity

- Examples:
    - None of the course countries analyzed have facist states
    - States with more authoritarian regimes, like Iran and Russia, may share some chararcteristics but are not correctly classified as facist


## 6. Populism
- -> Political philosophy that supports the interest and rights of the common people over those of the elites
- Core Features:
    - Challenges established political and economic structures perceived as favoring elites
    - Direct connection between leaders and the people, often bypassing traditional institutions
    - Can align with either liberal or conversative ideologies

- Philosophical Origins
    - 19th-Century agrarian movements advocating for the rights of farmers and workers
    - Modern politics as a response to perceived elite dominance and systemic inquality

- Examples:
    - Mexico
        - Presidents AMLO and Scheinbaum of Morena
    - Trump (USA), Orban (Hungary), Milei (Argetina), Meloni (Italy), Modi (India)
