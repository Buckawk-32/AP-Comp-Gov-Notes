## Electoral Systems & Voting Rules
- Electoral System -> System of voting and the rules therein
    - Determines how elections are run & how votes are counted

- Also determines:
    1. When elections occur
    2. Who can vote
    3. Who can run
    4. How ballots are cast & counted
    5. How votes determine the outcome
    6. Limits of campaign fundraising & spending


## Why Have All These Rules??
- All countries have electoral rules

- But their purpose depends on their regime type:
    - Democratic regimes:
        - Clear systems & rules remain *consistent*
            - Ensure free, fair, and competitive elections
    - Authoritarian Regimes:
        - Systems change frequently
            - Serves interests of those in charge
        - Examples:
            - [ Russia ](/Unit2/2.3and2.4.md#russia-semi-presidential)
            - [ China ](/Unit2/2.3and2.4.md#china-semi-presidential)
                - Change of term limits

## United Kingdom: Election System
- Remember: [UK: Bicameral Legislature](/Unit2/2.6.md#uk-bicameral-legislature)

- House of Commons members (Members of Parliament, MPs) are elected by:
    1. *Single-Member Districts* -> each electoral district has 1 officeholder. Multiple candidates, but just 1 member
    2. *First-Past-the-Post* -> candidates with the greatest number of votes is the winner. Not always > 50%

- Fixed-Terms Parliament Act
    - Elections required every 5 years at most

## Mexico
- Remember: [Mexico: Bicameral Legislature](/Unit2/2.6.md#mexico-bicameral-legislature) 

- Chamber of Deputies (Lower House)
    - 500 total members
        - 300 of which are elected by:
            1. SMDs and
            2. *Purality* -> Earn the largest number of votes
        - 200 of which are elected by:
            1. *Proportional Representation* -> Voters indicate their preference for a particular party. Parties then receive seatas in proporiton to their share of the vote
            2. *Multiple Member Districts* -> more than 1 rep per district
            - Advantage: Allows for diversity of parties
            - Most common system among world's democracies
    - Elections every 3 years

- Senate (Upper House)
    - 128 members
        - 32 by proportional representation
        - 96 from *Three-Seat Constituencies* -> each district elects 3 members
            - A form of MMDs
    - Elections every 6 years
    
- Districts with proporitonal representation in both the Chamber and Senate have gender quotas
    - A certain % of the party's candiates have to be women
        - Don't necessarily have to win
        - But have to be on the party list
    - Has increased female participation in the legislature

## Russia
- Remember: [Russia: Bicameral Legislature](/Unit2/2.6.md#russia-bicameral-legislature) 

- State Duma (Lower House)
    - 450 members
        - 225 from SMDs
        - 225 from proportional represenation with a *threshold* (-> minimum % needed for a party to gain seats)
            - 5%; guarantees that only parties with siginificant support get in
    - Elections every 5 years

- Federation Council (Upper House)
    - 2 from all 89 federal regions => 178 members
        - Appointed by reigonal authorities **or** elected by a regional legislature
        - No fixed terms -> serve as long as the authorities or regional legislature wants them there


## Iran
- Remember: [Iran: Unicameral Legislature](/Unit2/2.6.md#iran-unicameral-legislature) 

- Majles
    - 290 members
        - Must be vetted(approved) by the Guardian Council
    - Directly elected from both SMDs and MMDs
    - *Majority Voting System* -> Candidates must receivce > 50% of the vote to win
        - No Winner?
            - Runoff election
                - Losing candidates throws his voters to a candidate on the second round for some gain (back hand deals)
                - Skews/Corrupts elections
    - No political parties since all must follow Sharia law
    - Christians, Jews, non Shiite muslims have reserved seats
        - Baha'i do not
            - The Mormons of Islam


## China
- Remember: [China: Unicameral Legislature](/Unit2/2.6.md#china-unicameral-legislature) 

- National People's Congress
    - 2,980 members through an indirect elections process
        - Chosen by lower-level legislatures
    - Five-Tier Electoral System
        - Citizens vote for local People's Congresses at the county and townships levels
            - Elect NPC members for the region
    - Representation from Different Groups
        - Delegates come from provinces, autonomous regions, municipalities, the military, and special administrative regions (Hong Kong and Macau)
    - Ethnic minorities, professionals, and the CCP all have representation
    - Candidates are vetted by the CCP before being on the ballot
    - Five-Year terms, meet once per year
