## What is Democratization?
- Transition from an authoritarian to a democratic regime
- Trend towards into [(1.3) Features of a Democracy](/Unit1/1.3.md#features-of-democratic-regimes)
- Aims: 
    1. More competition, fairness, and transparent elections
    2. More citizen participation
    3. Universal suffrage for adults
    4. More transparency in government
    5. Protected civil liberties and rights
    6. Equal treatment of citizens
    7. Established rule of law


## Democratic Consolidation
- "Mature" Democracy / "Consolidated" Democracy
    - Democractic values are entrenched
    - Extremely unlikely to revert to authoritarianism without external influence    
        - Ex. UK

## Examples of Democratic Conslidation/How Democractic each Case Country is:

### Example 1: Mexico:
- 1929 ~ 2000: Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI)
    - Won all presidential elections
- 2000: PRI lost to National Action Party (PAN)
    - PRI gave up power
    - Feature of Democratic Regime
- 2018: President Nieto stepped down after his term limit
    - Example of Rule of Law
    - Feature of Democractic Regime

- Legislature: Congress of the Union
    - Elected officals
    - 50/50 men & women
    - Led by PRI and PAN predominantly, but legislative elections remain free, fair, and competitive

- Vote counting is open and results are transparent

- The Problem:
    - Things are changing (democratic backsliding)
    - 2018: accusations that the PRI were buying votes
    - Media control by Televisa 
        - Huge conglomerate that controls nearly all media 
        - Is very biased
        - Violence against other media outlets and journalists
            - more than 100 killed since 2000
    - Drug Cartels
        - Influence politics heavily
        - Affects local law enforcement
        - Leads to self-censorship & withdraw from participating
    - 2014: Mayor Jose Abarca
        - Cartel associate
        - Ordered attack on 43 students at a teaching college
    - Areas around the border are the worst
    - Leaders operate with impunity

### Example 2: Nigeria:
- Independent from Britain in 1960
    - Led mostly by military juntas
- Democratic in 1999 with a new constitution
    - President Olusegun Obasanjo elected from People's Democratic Party (PDP)
    - Served for two 4 year terms
    - Feature: Rule of Law, peaceful transistion of power

- 2015 election: First transfer of power between parties
    - PDP -> All Progressives Congress (APC)
    - Political participation increased dramatically from 1999
        - 93 mill registered voters in 2023
    - Current president: Bola Tinubu (APC)

- Challegenes to Democracy:
    - The same trends that happened before
    - Seperation of North and South, South -> PDP, North -> APC
        - Tinubu, is muslism, thus the South doesn't want to vote for him
        - PDP gets all the South, APC gets all the North

- Nigerian Elections:
    - No race, ethinic, gender, or religous requirement to run for office
    - Educational requirements (TO RUN THO), however
        - This limits people and draws people out of democracy
    - Voters must be:
        - Prior-registered
        - +18 years of age
        - Have a voter card (This costs money, thus it also limits people out)
            - Feature: 
    - 2006: Legislature rejected executive's push for a 3rd term
        - Feature: Checks and Balances

- Nigerian Problems?
    - Seperation of powers
        - 2019: Chief Justice of the Supreme Court suspended on the eve of Pres. election
    - Free Press
        - 2019: Newspaper published arrested for treason for "planning protests against the government"
            - Treason: plotting against the government
    - Electoral Security Forces
        - Meant to protect voting stations
        - But they are alleged to be intimators
