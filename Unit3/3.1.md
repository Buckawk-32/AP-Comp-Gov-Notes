## What is a Civil Society?
> [!INFO]
> Corresponded to the [ideas of John Locke](/Unit2/2.3and2.4.md#lockes-political-philosophy)

- In all societies, people have power:
    - To violently overhtrow their leaders

- In democratic societies, people have power:
    - To peacefully elect their leaders and peacefully change their government
    - ALso have other power to express their opinions and demand actions from the government

- -> the collective action of a state's citizens based upon shared interests, purposes, and values
    1. *Autonomous* from government *and* commercial for-profit companies
    2. *Voluntary* organizations
    3. *Groups* that help indivduals define, express, and advance their interests
    4. *Single Issue Focused*

## China: Weak & Underdeveloped
- Civil Society Status: Weak
    - Little evidence of civil society prior to:
        - Economic opening (late 1970s~80s)
        - Constitution of 1982
    - Some emerging NGOs

        - Enviromental causes
        - AIDs prevention
        - Legal reform

- Government Limits:
    - Control over media and internet
    - Religious groups are singled out and espeically controlled over
        - Falun Gong

- Current Civil Society Groups
    - Red Cross of China -> focused on disater relief
    - Village Committees -> responsible for local government & community development


## Iran: Weak/Extremely Limited
- Civil Society Status: Weak
    - No civil society under the Shah (pre-1979)
    - Some political liberalization in the late 90s
    - Crackdown from 2009 onwards

- Government Limites:
    - Government has limited public gathering, free speech, media
    - Arrests of those who speak out
    - However, large youth population that keep some civil society alive

- Current Civil Society Groups:
    - Basij Resistance Force and Kayhan -> mobilizes public support for the regime
        - They do counter protests against protests
    - Charity Foundation for Special Diseases -> helps families affected by disability
    

## Mexico: Developed/Growing
- Civil Society: Suprisingly Developed
    - 1 party rule for 70 years, but civil society always present
        - Reason: religion

- Government Limits:
    - Some limitations on assembly under PRI rule
    - PAN relaxed limits on civil society groups from 2009 onwards

- Current Civil Society Groups
    - Roman Catholic Church
    - Vecinos de la Colonia -> works to improve living conditions
    - Many NGOs focused on human rights
        - Result of violence and drug cartels
            - Government failure = civil society stepping up


## Nigeria: Weak & Underdeveloped
- Civil Society Status: Weak
    - No history of Civil Society under colonial era
    - Post-colonial: Interest groups grow to influence government
        - But seen as coercive or corrupt

- Government Limits:
    - Authoritarian history: colonial & military rule
    - Civil Society began emerging after 1999
    - 2017: government bill to limit NGOs and civil society
        - However never pased

- Current Civil Society Groups
    - MOSOP (Movement for thhe Survival of the Ogoni People) -> secures finnacial benefits for the Ogoni in the oil-rich Niger delta region
    - The Punch -> independent
    - etc etc


## Russia: Weak
- Civil Society Status: Weak
    - Little history of civil society under te USSR
    - Organized crime and eventually oligarchs control society in areas where government lacked
        - Because the organized crime isn't voluntary so it isn't a civil society

- Government Limits:
    - Tight control under Putin
    - Groups must register with the government
    - Treason laws expanded
    - RT-state funded television network

- Currect Civil Society Groups:
    - Eastern Orthodox Chruch -> Growing influence again, but seen as an extension of the Kremlin
    - Nashi: Pro-Kremlin youth movement that promotes patriotism & loyality 
        - Still considered a civil society, as they check all the boxes
        - *Example of how Authoritiatrian regimes use Civil Society to their advantage*


## United Kingdom: Developed
- Civil Society Status: Developed
    - Long history of civil society
        - Beginning in the 1820s~1830s with liberal movements and organized labor

- Government Limits:
    - Government supports & protects
    - Ingrained in the culture

- Current Civil Society Groups:
    - Church of England -> Protestant Christain Chruch 
    - BBC -> News Media/Non Profit
    - Greenpeace -> enviromental issues
    - Oxfam -> alleviate poverty and injustice
