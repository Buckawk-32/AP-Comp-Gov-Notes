## Social/Economic Equality in Different Regimes
- Social Welfare State -> a government system that provide economic and social security for its citizens
    - Government or institutions play a central role in protecting and promoting citizens' well-being
    - Public housing, education, and pensions

- Similarites
    - Both democratic & authoritarian regimes can provide forms of social and economic equality
        - Examples include housing, education, and retirement benefits

- Differences
    - Authoritarian Regimes
        - Equality often proivdev as a means to maintain control and stability
        - State retains significant power over how resource and distributed
    - Democratic Regimes
        - Equality is shaped by laws ensuring citizens participation and accountability
        - Social welfare programs are more likely to reflect public demands


## Post-Materialism
- -> Valuing self-expression and quality of life over material needs
    - Leads to pressure on governments to address enviromental issues, social equality, and economic equality
    - Becomes prominent in societies with economic stability

- Enviromental Issues: China vs. UK
    - China
        - Focus on enviromental issues has grown since 2017 due to citizen protests
        - Government tightly controls when and how these issues are addressed
        - Crackdowns on protest and independent enivormental campaigns reflect authoritarian rule

    - UK
        - Citizens and NGOs address enviromental issues freely, without government suppression
        - Demostrates alingment with democratic prinicples of protexted rights and civil society engagement
