## International vs. Supranational Organizations
- International Organizations
    - -> organizations where countries work together but retain sovereignty
        - *World Bank* -> provides loans to developing countries to help them promotes growth and development
        - *International Monetary Fund (IMF)* -> promotes financial stability and the conditions for nations to grow economically
        - *United Nations (UN)* -> serves to promote cooperation between countries and promote international secutiy, human rights, and development

    - IMF & World Bank Aid
        - Allow for more opportunity for global trade while providing for domestic aid
        - But to have strong conditions/strings attached
            - IMF's *Structural Adjustment Program* (SAP) requires states to:
                1. Reduce *deficit spending*
                2. Privatize state-owned enterprises
                3. Reduce both tariffs and domestic subsidies
                4. Cut government spending on social services

## SAP in Nigeria (1986)
- Response to mounting debt + decline in oil prices
    1. Devalue of the currency to boost exports
    2. Liberalized trade barriers and tariffs
    3. Austerity

- Led to economic improvement but increased inequality and inflation


## SAP in Russia (1991)
- Move away from command economy following the collapse of the USSR
    1. Demand economy
    2. Massive privatization

- Led to rapid economic transformation & rise of a private sector
    - But is associated with a rise in inequality & oligarchy

2. What are the mainbodies of the United Nations, and what roles do they play in global governance?
    - General Assembly -> All members
        - Primary delibrating body, highest form of policy making
            - Deciding memberships, world peace
        - Needs 2/3 approval
        - Resolutions are based on good faith

    - Security Council
        - Maintaining international peace and stoping world war
            - Has been effective in keeping war small and regional
        - Rotation of 15 varing countries (Big or Small)
            - 5 Major Powers:
                - China, US, UK, France, Russia
            - 10 Rotating members
        - Needs All 5 Major Powers and majority of non power nations
        - Security Council resolutions are legally binding

    - Economic and Social Council
        - Jurisdiction over economic, social welfare, culture and the enviroment
            - 30 regional subsidaries

    - Institution of Justice
        - Main judicial body, State vs. State
            - 15 jugdes, nominated
                - Has to be from different states
        - Relying on state consent for a state to appear in front of the Int. of Justice

    - The UN Secretariat
        - Responsible for day to day business
            - Ex. Filing papers, office work
            - Assisted by 1000s of UN staff members

    - Trusteeship Council
        - Supervise the 11 UN territories
            - Former colonies
                - Helping them stand on themselves


3. How does the UN influence the policies and actions of its member states?

## International vs. Supranational Organizations
- Supranational Organizations
    - -> organizations where countries give up sovereignty for collective decision making
    - Ex. 
        - *World Trade Organization* -> promotes international trade and facilitates it through the adoption of rules

        - *European Union (EU)* -> promotes union and economic growth between nation states
            - Currency (Euro)
                - Removing barriers to trade by giving the same currency
                - Separte political structure that serves to create and reinforce law on these European states
                    - European parliament, European Commission, and European Council

        - *Economic Community of West Africa States (ECOWAS)* -> promotes economic integration among member states
            - Undergoes peacekeeping missions
                - Nigeria: largest economy and population
                    - Uses it's military and oil to influence the organizaiton 
            - Discussion of a common currency 


## Import Substitution vs. Global Trade
- *Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI) Model*
    - -> economic policy where a country reduces dependency on foreign imports by promoting domestic production of goods
    - Tariffs + subsidies + and nationalization
        - Opposite of economic liberliazation

- Ex.
    - Mexico (1940s ~ 1980s): goal of developing domestic industries
        - Economic crises of the 1980s forced Mexico to take loans from the IMD and World Bank, leading to economic liberalization
        - Joined NAFTA in 1994
    - Nigeria (1960s ~ early 1980s)
        - Oil price crashes in the late 1970s
        - Took IMG loans, leading to SAPs in the mid-1980s that ended ISI policies
