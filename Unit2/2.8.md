# Judicial Systems

## Objective
1. Describe the structure and functions of judiciaries


## Functions of a Judiciary
- 5 main funcitons or duties:
    1. Interpret and apply laws
    2. Resolve disputes
    3. Uphold rights and liberties of citizens
    4. Maintain a seperation of powers
    5. Establish rule of law through precedent

- Not all judiciaries perform all 5 functions


## The Origins of Law
- Earliest written legal codes appear in ancient Mesopotamia around 2000 BCE
    - Ex. Code of Ur-Nammu and the Code of Hammurabi
        - Introduced proportional punishment and standardized rules for settling disputes


## The Legacy of Ancient Legal Codes
- Established the concept of written laws
- Influenced later legal systems (e.g. Roman Law, Justinian Code)
    - Introduced principles still relevant today
        - Ex. Presumption of Innocence


## Development of Legal Traditions
- Ancient Greece: Contributed concepts of democracy and citzenship
- Roman Law: Developed comprehensive legal system
- Islamic Law: Sharia Law based on Quran and Hadith
- English Common Law: Judge-made law based on precendent
- Civil Law: Codified statues, orginated in continental Europe 
    - Also called Napoleonic Code


## Legal History: United Kingdom
- Common law tradition dating back to the 11th century
    - Magna Carta (1215): Limited royal power, established rule of law
    - Bill of Rights (1689): Futher limited moarchy, strengthened Parliament


## Modern UK Judicial Systems
- 2 primary court structures:
    1. Court of original jurisdiction
    2. Appeals Courts

- District courts hear cases that can be appealed to the High Courts
    - Before 2009: High Courts = Law Lords
        - Members of the House of Lords
        - Settled disputes from lower courts

- 2009: Supreme Court replaced the Law Lords
    - President & 11 justices appointed by a panel of lawyers


## UK Judicial Quirks
- UK still uses *common law*
    - Unwritten laws and principles based on legal precendents
        - Judges have power of discretion over vague laws
        - Interpretation of these laws sets precedents

- Remember: UK is governed by the idea of *parliamentary sovereignty*
    - That is, parliamen's desicions are final
    - Supreme Court *does not* have the ability to nullify law
    - Has the authority to determive if the government's decision violates common law traditions
- Supreme Court also hears cases related to disputes about distribution of power from central gov. to regional (devolved power)
    - With the exception of immigration law, there are different court systems within the UK:
        1. English and Wales
        2. Scotland
        3. Northern Ireland


## UK Example: Miller I (2016) - Brexit and Parliament's Role
- Background
    - Case arose after the 2016 Brexit referendum (-> direct vote by the public on specific legislation)
        - Results of the referendum: British people voted to leave the European Union
            - David Cameron resigned as a result of his support to Remain

    - Prime Minister Theresa may takes over, supports Leave
        - Planned to trigger Article 50 using prerogative powers (-> traditional executive powers once held by the monarch)
            - Article 50: Any EU member state can decide to leave the EU
        - Claimants argued Brexit would alter domestic rights and therefore required an act of Parliment

- UK Supreme Court Ruling
    - Government cannot trigger Article 50 without an act of Parliament
    - Reaffirmed that Parliament has final authority over major constitutional changes
    - Demonstrated the Supreme Court's role as a check on executive power
- Why This Case Matters
    1. Shows how the post-2009 Supreme Court interprets constituional prinicples
    2. Highlights tension between executive authority and parliamentary sovereignty
    3. Clear example of judicial review in a common law system without a single written constitution

### Result: UK's Seperation of Powers
- Miller 1 revealed a few truths about UK institutions:
    1. Reaffirmed parliamentary sovereignty, meaning Parliament not the Prime Minister -- has final authority
    2. Showed that the Supreme Court can check the executive, even in a system without a codified constitution


## Legal History: Mexico
- Pre-Colonial: Aztec legal system based on oral traditions
- Colonial Era: Spanish civil law introduced
- Independence (1810): Adopted Spanish *civil* law system with some common law influences
- Current system based on 1917 Constitution
- Recent reforms aim to improve transparency and efficieny
       

## Modern Mexican Legal System
- 2 Court System
    - Federal and State
    - Most laws are federal 
        - So federal courts has *supremacy*

- Courts of Origination
    - Where trials begin
    - Federal and State

- Courts of Appeals
    - Where you can appeal verdicts
    - Federal and State


## Mexican Supreme Court
- Divided
    - Plenum (Full Court): Made up of 11 justices
    - Chambers: 5 justices x 2, 1 determining justice
- Justicies are nominated by the President
- Confirmed by the Senate
- Serve for 12 years
- By 2025,
    - Now they are elected, by popular vote

- Main function: *judicial review*
    - Deteremine if laws are constitutional or not


## Legal History: Nigeria
- Pre-colonial: Customary laws varied by ethinic group
- Colonial Era: English common law introduced
- Independence (1960): Adopted common law system
- Also incorporates customary law and Sharia law in some states (particularly in the North)
    - Ex. Female gentailia mutaliation, Adultery


## Modern Nigerian Legal System
- 1999 Constitution established:
    - Unified court of origination at federal and state levels
    - Federal Court of Appeals
    - Supreme Court

- States can also set up or authorize traditional subsidiary courts
    - Sharia Courts in Nothern Nigeria


## Nigerian Supreme Court
- Power of judicial review
    - *Appellate jurisdiction* (for the most part)
    - But ALSO(!) has *original jurisdiction* in state vs state and state vs federal cases

- 21 justices
    - Recommended by a judicial council
    - appointed by the President
    - confirmed by the Senate

- 72 federal appeals courts across the country
    - Hear appeals from state courts of origination and appeals


## Legal History: Russia
- Pre-Soviet:
    - Tsarsit legal system influcened by Byzantine and Western European law
- Soviet Era (1917 ~ 1991):
    - Socialist legal system, party control over courts
- Post-Soviet:
    - Return to civil law system, ongoing reforms
- Constitiutional Court established iin 1993


## Modern Russian Legal System
- Prior to 1993 -- Kangaroo Courts (Pre-determined Court in which the outcome is rigged)
    - Used and manipulated by the Communist Party
    - Show trials (to please the public, fake trials)

- Constitutional Court established in 1993
    - 19 members appointed by the President, confirmed by the Federation Council
    - Responsible for judicial review (power to review laws),

- Supreme Court was also created by the 1993 constitution
    - 115 members appointed by the President and confirmed by the Federation Council
        - This means that the president has full control over the sumpreme court (Federation Council is appointed by the President)
    - Hears cases that begin with the District Courts and move through the Appeals Courts
    - Has final say on Russian *administrative, civil, and criminal law*


## Constitutional Court vs Supreme Court in Russia
1. Jurisdiction & Focus
    - Constitutional Court: constitutional law, reviewing cases tat involve potential conflicts with the Russian Constituion
    - Supreme Court: oversees lower court's rulings and ensures uniform application of federal law acorss the judiciary; applies rather than interprets laws

2. Scope
    - Constituional Court: authority to strike down laws or government acts it finds unconstitutional
    - Supreme Court: sets legal precedents and ensures consistency in the application of laws across the country


## Legal History: China
- Ancient China: 
    - Legalist and Confucian influenes on law
- Imperial Era: Codfified legal system, but emphasis on moral education
- Mao Era: Socialist legal system, often arbitrary
- Post-1980: Legal reform, but stil to Communist Party Control


## Modern Chinese Legal System
- 1980: Organic Law of the People's Courts
- Civil law system
- 4 levels of the Court System:
    - First 3 handle both criminal and civil cases & divided on the basis of adminstrative level
        1. Primary - Counties, Towns, Municipal Districts
        2. Intermediates - Prefectures & municipalities
            - Original jurisdiction for serious cases
            - Appellate jurisdiction from appealed Primary Court cases
        3. High - Province-level. Handle appeals from Intermediate Courts and serious province-wide matters
    - 4th one is for nationwide & Politically sensitive cases
        **4.** Supreme People's Court 
            - 400 judges who serve 5 year terms

- Theoretically, SPC is subject to the NPC
    - Remember, NPC is supreme institution according to the constitution
    - Therefore, SPC has no right to *judicial review*

- Like the NPC, judiciary is subservient to the CCP
 

## Legal History: Iran
- Pre-Islamic: *Zorostrian* influences on law
- Islamic conquest (7th century): Introduction of Sharia Law
- 1906 Constitution: Attempt to mdoernize legal system
- 1979 Islamic Revolution: Return to Sharia-based system
- Current system combines Islamic law with aspects of Civil law


## Modern Iranian Legal System
- Not *secular*
    - Neither Common Law nor Civil Law

- Judges are trained in Sharia Law
    - More like civil law in that there are many codifications

- Multi-layer system headed by a chief justice who is a *cleric*
    - -> religious scholar
    - Chief justice => 5 year term appointed by the [Supreme Leader](/Unit2/2.3and2.4.md#iran-parilamentary)
        - Can nominate half of the Guardian Council

- Below the Chief Justice is the Supreme Court
    - 42 branches
    - Every branch has a justice, judge, and magistrate

- Function of the courts: Judicial review to ensure laws do not violate Sharia Law
- Revolutionary Courts handle national security matters
    - Spies and such
