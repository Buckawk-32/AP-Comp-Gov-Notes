## Unicameral vs Bicameral vs Hybrid Parliament
- Cameral -> a legislative "chamber"
    - Part of legislature

- Unicameral Legislature -> A legislature with 1 chamber
    - Ex. China and Iran
    - Advantages:
        1. Simplicity -> just one set of rules to follow
        2. Effeciency -> fewer chance for the chambers to disagree
        3. Cheaper -> Smaller staff & use of resources
        4. Representation -> Easier to hold members accountable

- Bicameral Legislature -> A legislature with 2 chambers 
    - Ex. Britain, Mexico, Nigeria
    - Advantages:
        1. Checks & Balances -> Less "bad laws" since a bill needs to pass both houses
        2. Diverse Viewpoints -> More representation from people of all backgrounds
        3. Greater Representation -> Chambers may be designed to represent different groups
        4. Greater Stability -> Fewer major changes, more predictability

- Hybrid adavntages
    - Remember: Semi-Presidential
        - President elected by the people
        - President appoints the PM

    1. Checks -> President can check the PM and the Legislature
    2. Reponsiveness -> More responsive to the people's concerns


## China: Unicameral Legislature
- National People's Congress (NPC)
    - 3000 members indirectly "elected" through a series of Communist legislative assemblies
    - Members serve 5 year terms
    - Meets for a 2 week session each March
    - Reconginzed by the Constitution as the most powerful institution
        - Ability to choose the President (Xi Jin Ping) and Vice President (Han Zheng)
        - Approves the Preemier (Head of Government)
        - Controlled by the CCP
        - All legislation is a rubber stamp of the Party's initiatives which come from the Politburo

    - Politburo
        - Highest political body of the Central Committee of the CCP
            - Basically Politburo makes all the desicions
            - Tells the NPC what to pass
        - *NOT a legislature*


## Iran: Unicameral Legislature
- The Majles
    - Created by the Constitution of 1906
        - Remember: Iranian Revolution happened in 1979!
        - Majles survived Shah's (King) dicatorships, WWII, and the coup
    - Retained after 1979
    - 290 seats, directly elected through *single-member districts*
        - -> electoral district that sends a single representative back to a multi-representative body
    - 4 year fixed terms whose elections are separate from presidential elections
    - Has the power to:
        1. enact laws
        2. interprets laws
        3. appoints 6 of the 12 members of the Guardian Council 
        4. remove cabinet members
        5. and approve bugets, appointments, treaties, and loans

    *BUT*
    - Guardian Council vets members and laws to make sure they are in line with Sharia Law
        - Sharia Law -> religious regulations governming the lives of Muslims
        - 6 of the 12 are nominated by the SL; other 6 from the judiciary
        - Majles vote to approve appointments


## UK: Bicameral Legislature
- 2 chambers/house with distinct functions
    - Unequal in power

- House of Commons: Lower House
    - 650 members who are directly elected
    - Seat of power in British system
        - 1911 and 1949 Parliamentary Acts removed the powers of the Upper House
            - House of Lords can no longer veto legislation of the House of Commons, only delay
            - House of Lords also cannot amend Money Bills
        - Assumptions in UK regime:
            - 1 party will win a majority of seats
            - The other party (minority) will check the majority

- House of Lords: Upper House
    - Original Parliament
        - Lost power after the English Civil War and Glorious Revolution
    - 775 appointed seats
    - Only hereditary house in the world today
        - Before the 20th century, the House of Lords and its 775 seats consisted of hereditary peers (about one-half of the total number seats) and life peers
        - Hereditary peers mean indivduals who hold a seat that has been passed down through family
        - A life peer is a seat where the indivudal was appointed because of life long or distinguished service
        - *The House of Lords are not elected by the people*
    - Today, only 92 of the seats are hereditary
        - 2 main functions today -> delay legislation and debate technicalities of proposed bills


## Mexico: Bicameral Legislature
-  2 houses/chambers that are directly elected by the people 

- Chamber of Deputies: Lower House
    - 500 members with 3 year terms
        - 300 from single-member districts, 200 by proportional representation
            - -> system in which parties gain seats in proportion to the number of votes cast for them
        - Approves legislation, levies taxes, verfies election results

- Senate: Upper House
    - 128 members with 6 year terms
        - 96 are directly elected from 31 states + 1 federal territory
        - 32 by proportional representation
    - Pass legislation, confirm presidential appointments to the Supreme Court, approve treaties, and approve federal intervention in state matters


## Nigeria: Bicameral Legislature
- 2 houses/chambers that are directly elected by the people

- House of Representatives: Lower House
    - 360 members from single-member-districts
    - Elected by purality -> simple majority, even if it's not above 50%
    - Represents local interests
    - Can pass laws

- Senate: Upper House 
    - 109 senators, three from each of 36 states, and one from the federal captial terrioty of Abuja
    - Represents the staes' interests
    - Can pass laws and has the power to impeach


## Russia: Bicameral Legislature
- 2 chambers/houses

- But... Russian President has the power of decree
    - State Duma: Lower House
        - 450 directly elected members
            - 225 seats through proportional representation
            - 225 through direct majoritiy elections (1 deputy per constituency)
        - Pass legislation, confirming presidential appointees, approve the budget
            - However, remember that the executive branch is more powerful under Putin and that Putin's political party dominates the number of seats in the Duma (340/450)

    - Federation Council: Upper House
        - 2 members from each of the 89 federal regions
             - 1 representative is selected by the governor of the region and 1 representative is selected by the regional legislature
