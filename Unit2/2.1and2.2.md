# Parliamentary, Presidential, and Semi Presidential Systems - A Comparison

## Learning Objectives
1. Describe parliamentary, presidential, and semi-presidential systems
2. Compare institutional relations among pariamentary, presidential, and semi-presidental systems


## Parliamentary Systems
- Combine the legislative and executive functions
- National legislature selects and removes the cabinet and head of government
    - Cabinet -> Ministers that advise the executive
        - Finance Minister, Defense Minister, etc.
    - Head of Government -> Chief executive in charge of running the daily affairs of government
        - Prime Minister, President, etc.
- Head of government in this system -> Prime Minister
- United Kingdom
    - Legislature's majority party selects the PM, not the citizens
    - Cabinet -> includes cabinet members & head of bureaucracies
        - Center of policy making
        - Collective responsibility

- Terms to know:
    - PM => Prime Minister
    - MP => Member of Parliamentary Systems
    - HM => His/Her Majesty's Government

- Parliamentary Elections
    - Exletions are a futher check on the PM's power
        - UK now at fixed term elections every 5 years for the House of Commons
            - Began in 2011 -> Fixed Terms Parilament Act

- PM may also call snap elections
    - -> early election
    - The Reason?:
        - Force all MPs to run for re-election after approval for the monarch
        - Election held 30 days after the call
        - PM would only do this with a supermajority, super high poll numbers, OR if they know their poll numbers will continue to fall
    - 2017: Theresa May's suprising snap elections backfired
        - She thought she could get a majority win
    - 2019: Boris Johnson won them back with a snap elections for Brexit
    - 2024: Rishi Sunak calls early election, leads to Labour Victory
        - He knew the people were tired of his party, so he cut his losses, also because of economic stagnation

## Presidential Systems
- Separate legislative and executive functions
- Cabinet that is mostly responsible to the elected executive
    - Cabinet is nominated by the President and then confirmed by the legislative
- Legislatures that confirm cabinet members and that can remove cabinet members through impeachment
- Separate fixed-term, popular elections for executive and legislative
    - Ex. Executive -> 4 year terms or 6 year terms
    - Ex. Legislative/House of Represenatives -> 2 year terms

- Top executive is head of state and head of government
- Legislative functions
    - Create laws, impose taxes, declare war, ratify/reject treaties

- Mexico
    - Executive/Head of State/Head of Government/Commander in Chief -> Claudia Sheinbaum (Morena)
        
- Nigeria
    - Executive/Head of State/Head of Government/Commander in Chief -> Bola Tinubu (APC)

- Reason for President to be Commander in Chief:
    - If the military is left by themselves, then they can really easliy impose martial law, and control the government

- With 3 separate branches
    1. Executive
    2. Legislative
    3. Judicary
        - Each branch constantly checks each other


## Semi-Presidential System
- Also called a "dual executive" system
- President + Prime Minister and Cabinet
- Separate popular elections for president national legislature
    - President then appoints a prime minister
- Elements of both systems
    1. Parliamentary -> PM and cabinet handle legislation of the state
    2. Presidential -> popularly elected

- Russia
    - President is elected by direct voting, President is also the Commander in Chief
    - PM is appointed by the president with consent of Legislature (Duma)
        - Had very little power until 2008 when Putin became PM
            - Because once Putin had already served 2 terms as President, his puppet became President and put Putin as Prime Minister
        - Once his puppet stepped down, Putin returned to power as President
            - Different than other semi systems (France & India)


## Comparison Table

|Parts of Comparison| Parliamentary System | Presidential System | Semi-Presidential System |
|---|---|---|---|
|Branches| No separation of powers | Separation of powers | Separation of powers |
|Power| Lies with the legislature | Specific powers within each branch | Specific powers within each branch |
|Elections| Legislature elected by the people directly | Executive and legislature branches voted on by the people directly | Executive branches voted on by the people directly/President appoints prime Minister and cabinet |
|Roles| Prime Minister | President/Legislature | President/Prime Minister coexist |
|Function| Typically less gridlock | Gridlock is a common problem | Little gridlock as Prime Minister is typically an adminstriative role |


## Checks and Balances
- Parilamentary Systems
    - Fewer Checks
        - Easier to enact policy
    - Legislative power to check the executive:
        1. Censure -> Formal statement of disapproval
        2. Refusal to pass legislation
        3. Direct questioning of the cabinet or PM
            - Happens regularly
            - Opposition questioning: verbally challenger the majority's desicions
            - Majority questioning: represent local constituents

- Presidential Systems
    - Equal branches of government check each other
