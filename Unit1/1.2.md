- States
    - Nations
        - Regimes
            - Governments


## To be a State, a country must have:
1. Territory -> an area of land and defined borders

2. People -> a permanent population that lives within a territory

3. Sovereingty -> independent legal authority over the population of a territory
    - Both internal and external
        - Internal = e.g. taxes
        - External = e.g. treaties
    - No sovereignty = no autonomy
    - No autonomy = failed state

4. Government -> institutions or indivduals legally empowered to make descisions for a state
    - Implied: right to use power to enforce policies & decisions


## Nations
A group of people with commonalities
- Race, Languages, Religions, ethnicity, Shared Identity, etc
- Self identified
- States are often run by Nations, making them a Nation-State
- Not all states are nation-states
    - Ex. Tamils in Sri Lanka
        - Tamils are a nation of people, but they don't run the state of Sri Lanka
    - Some might consider the nation as oppressed, as they don't run their own state

## Features of Democracies
### Regimes
- The fundamental rules that control access to and the exercise of political power
    - Sometimes establised by a founding document?
        - Ex. UK
    - Endure from government to government

- Regime --> Democracy -> Constitutional Monarchy, Republic
          -> Autocracy

- States --> Regimes -> Democracy, Authoritarian --> Government

### Example: Regime vs Government
- US Consitituion (Stays the same through different governments)
    - The government changes leaders and such

- Japan Regime -> Still a Monarchy but a Constitutional Monarchy
    - Government is still a democracy
