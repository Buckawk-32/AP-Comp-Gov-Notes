# Political Responses to Global Market Forces

## Learning Objectives:
1. Compare *political responses* to global market forces
2. Explain the *implications of the similarities and differences* between countries with different political systems, priniciples, institutions, processes, policies, and behaviors

## Market Forces
- *Capital* -> liquid assets; cash in hand
- *Private vs. Public Ownership*
    - *Private* -> indivudals or entities who won exclusive rights to assets, business, or properties, allowing for autonomous decision making
    - *Public* -> collective ownership by either the government or public at large with broader societal goals and often subject to public oversight and regulation

- *Privatization* -> transferring of state-owned enterprises to private owners
    - Used to increase efficiency and cut government spending
- *Nationalization* -> transferring of private enterprises to the government
    - Used to increase social welfare, protect national interests, and regulate the economy


## UK
- Mixed economy -> economic system that incoroporates elements of both market economies (capitalism) and command economies (socialism)
    - Socialist elements: NHS, progressive tax, public education

- World's largest exporter of financial services
    - Traders catch the end of the day in Asia + the beginning of the trading in NYC
    - London Stock Exchange
    - Major international banks (HSBC, Barclays)

- Privatized national resources, especially after the 1980s
    - British Petroleum, Coal, etc.
    - Some rail systems
    - Are heavily regulated however


## China
- Export-oriented growth since the 1980s (Deng Xiaoping)
    - "World's factory"
    - Privatization and economic liberalization
        - No privatization of natural resources, however

- Establishment of *Special Economic Zones* (SEZs)
    - Primarily along the east coast
    - Features:
        1. Low minimum wage for workers
        2. Reduced tax rates for foreign investors
        3. No tariffs on imported industrial materials
        4. Som autonomy from central government

- *Belt and Road Initiative* (BRI)
    - Aimed at enhancing trade, promoting culture


## Mexico
- Economic integration & dependency on NA neighbors
    - NAFTA, USMCA
    - *Maquiladoras* -> foreign-owned factories with tariff free imports of raw materials
        - Use of cheap labor
    - Resulted in uneven geographic development

- Reliance on natural resources (Ex. Oil)
    - Petroleos Mexicanos (PEMEX)
        - Used to be state-owned; privatized starting in 2015
        - Allows for more competition in the oil and gas industry in Mexico


## Nigeria
- Natural resource dependent
    - Cocoa + rubber

- Oil discovered in the 1970s
    - Led to the creation of the *Nigerian National Petroleum Corporation* (NNPC)
        - State-owned but partners with foreign private private MNCs like BP and Shell
            - Joint ventures to attract foreign investment and promote new technologies, while also retaining some domestic control
            - Resulting economic growth


## Russia
- Export reliant
    - Arms & milittary equipment since the end of the Cold War
    - Oil/Gas = 17% of GDP

- Instance of *re-nationalization*
    - Yukos (oil company) integrated into the state-owned company Gazprom, after the company's founder was accused of fraud and corruption 
        - Gazprom => state-owned
    - Putin prefers to limit foreign control of natural resources and FDI
        - Security purposes

- Economic sanctions since 2014 have hurt FDI


## Iran
- Connected with global oil markets
    - Constant economic fluctuations
    
- Lack of transparency in state-owned enterprises
    - Detrimental

- Trying to move away from oil dependency
    - Manufacturing & technology
    - Sanctions hurt, however
    - 2026 War will hurt even more


## Government Responses to Market Forces
- Different responses depends on their goals

- Example Goal 1: Improve domestic conditions
    - Nigeria -> protection of oil industry to ensure demoestic growth

- Example Goal 2: Control or influence on domestic political demates to maintain or increase power
    - Iran -> tight countrol over industry reduces reliance on foreign imports & keep authoritarian regimes intact

- Example Goal 3: Extend national fluence internationally and/or demostically
    - Mexico -> privatization of PEMEX makes it industry move competitive globally

- Example Goal 4: Respond to domestic demands
    - UK -> demands from the public to reduce the budget and increase competition

- Example Goal 5: Provide resource security 
    - Russia & China -> both feel threatened by the West; control of natural resources
