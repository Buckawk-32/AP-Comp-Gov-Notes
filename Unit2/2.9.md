## Independence
- Independence -> especially of political institutions -> is linear and relative
- The degree of independence depends on the following factors:
    1. Authority to Overrule. Can the judiciary overrule the legislative and executive?
        - No nullfication = less indepedence
    2. Appointment Process. How does a judge gets his/her job?
        - Appointment without senate approval = less independence
    3. Term Limits. How long can a judge remain in office?
        - Term Limits = less independence
    4. Professional and Academic Backgrounds. What did the judge do before being all judgy?
        - No law degrees = less independence
    5. Impeachment Process. How easily can a judge be removed from office?
        - Easy impeachment = less independence

## What is Appellate Jurisdiction?
- The authority of a higher court to review and change decisions made by a lower court
    - Also called Court of Appeals

## Funcitons of an Independent Judiciary
1. Maintain checks and balances
2. Protect civil rights and liberties
3. Establish rule of law
4. Maintain separation of powers
5. Hold the government accountable


## Undermining the Judiciary
- *All* legislature and executive branches try to undermine the judiciary in various way
    - From established democracies to new dictatorships

- Then try to...
    1. Appoint loyal judges; removing challengning judges
    2. Blackmail or intimidate
    3. Ignore or refuse to implement rulings
    4. Limit jurisdiction
    5. Allowing police intervention
    6. Controlling professional associations
    7. Rule by decree
