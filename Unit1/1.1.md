## Analyzing Data:
- 2 types of data:
    - Quantitative -> Numbers based, countable, measurable
        - Most often uses numerical/empirical data
        - Says: What is happening
    - Qualitative -> Interpretation based, descriptive, relating to language
        - The same people can have 2 different stories based on which side you are on
        - Often referred to as normative information
            - Information that is the norm in different backgrounds
            - Ex. Taiwan is a country by Taiwan's background, Taiwan isn't a country by China's background
            - Value judgements & descriptive data


## Quantitative Data:
- Charts, tables, and graphs
    - Used to make inference about and comparisons between our 6 countries to:
        - Deteremine which countries are democractice or authoritarian
        - Compare presidential vs parliamentary systems
        - Evaluate interactions between branches of government
        - Analyze voting and political participation of different groups


## Qualitative Data:
- Speechs, foundational documents, political cartoons, maps and political commentaries
    - Can also be used to make inference and comparisons about all of the above

### Independent Variable:
- Variable that you control

### Dependent Variable:
- Variable that is the output of the Independent Variable measured


# KEY IDEA:
In political science, we study how changes in independent variables affect dependent variable
- Example: Independent variable -> Electoral system
           Dependent variable -> Number of politcal parties


## Correlation vs Causation:
Correlation -> a connection between 2 or more things
- Exisits when there is an association between 2 or more variables

Causation -> the action of causing something
- Diffcult to determine with certainty in the social sciences (or any science for that matter!)


## How We Measure and Collect Data:
We as political scientists will look at different data sets to compare our 6 countries
1. GDP
2. GDP per Capita
3. GDP Growth Rate
4. Human  Development Index (HDI)
5. Gini Index
6. Freedom House Index
7. Transparency International
8. Fragile States Index

### GDP (Gross Domestic Product):
Total market value of all goods and services produced in an economy in a year
- Economic Growth Rate
    - % change in GDP
- GDP per Capita
    - Economic output per person (GDP / population -> Mean GDP)
- Found from the IMF, CIA World Factbook

### HDI (Human Development Index):
A measure of average achievement in key dimensions of human development
- Usually includes life expectancy, amount of schooling, and income
    - Expressed as a value between 0 (low) and 1 (high)
- Found at UN Development Programme

### GINI Index (Coefficient / Ratio):
Measure income inequality within a country
- A Gini of 100% indicates perfect inequality whereas a Gini of 0% indicates perfect equality
- Found at the World Bank

### Freedom House Index:
Ranks countries based on scores for political rights (0 ~ 40) and civil liberties (0 ~ 60)
- 0 = least free, 100 = super free
- Found at Freedom House

### Transparency International:
Governmental Transparency -> ability of citizens to access information about a government's policy making and policy implementation to help hold officals accountable
- Transparency International produces the Corruption Perception Index
    - 0 = completely corrupt
    - 100 = transparent
- Found at Transparency International

### Fragile States Index:
Assesses and ranks countries based on their potential to weaken due to conflicts and domestic turmoil
- 12 total indicators grouped as:
    - cohesion indicators
    - economic indicators
    - political indicators
    - social / cross cutting indictors
- Scored as  0~120
    - Higher the score, more fragile the country
- Found at Fragile States
