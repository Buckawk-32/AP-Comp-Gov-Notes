# Pluralist and Corporatist Interests 

## Learning Objectives:
1. Describe *pluralist and corporatist interest group* systems

## Pluralist vs. Corporatist Systems
- *Pluralist Systems* -> promote competition among autonomous groups not linked to the state
    - Power is split among many groups that compete for the chance to influence the government's decision making
    - Encourage voluntary discussion & autonomy from government control
    - Emphasize equal access to the political process
        - Irregardless of size or resources
    - No single group dominates the decision-making process

- *Corporatist System* -> government controls access to policy-making by relying on state-sanctioned groups or single peak associations (SPAs) to represent labor, business, and agricultural sectors
    - SPAs -> organizations that represent a specific sector or industry
    - Centralized decision making
    - Limits participation to a few groups
    - State retains control over citizen input


## Pluralist <-> Corporatist
- When democracies move from *pluralism* to *corporatism*, this is a sign it is becoming more authoritarian
    - Corportatist system: state remains more control over citizen input
        - Interest groups only come through state approval
    - Ex. Iran
        - Islamic Labor Councils

- Alternatively, emerging democracies will move from corporatism to pluralism
    - Ex. Mexico
        - Dominated by PRI until 2000
            - Divided interest groups into three sectors: labor, peasent, and the middle class which each dominated by PRI-controlled groups
        - PAN began to challenge and criticize the PRI and its power, PRI's control over interest groups began to fade
        - Now in a period of transition
    - Ex. UK and the Confederation of British Industry (CBI)
        - Independent from government control
