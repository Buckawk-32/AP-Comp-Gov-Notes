## Single Member vs. Multi-Member Districts
- Electoral Systems
    - SMDs -> One representative per constituency
    - MMDs -> Multiple representatives elected, often based on party votes


## Proportional Representation
- -> Parties appoint legislative members *proportional* to election Results
- *Benefits:*
    - Increase in the number of parties represented
    - Increase in the election of women & minorites
        - *These benefits are amplified in systems with MMDs!*

- *Drawback:*
    - Proliferation of small parties and coalitions
        -  Hard to form stable governments
    - Small parties can and often do handcuff legislation since they can't pass their own legislation


## Plurality and SMD Systems
- One candidates with the most votes wins the sole seat
- Promotes 2 dominant parties
- Strong *constituency service*
    - -> being answerable to the citizens who elected you

- Ensures geographic represenation
    - Views vary depending on the country


## Presidential Systems
- Mexico
    - President elected through plurality of the popular vote

- Nigeria
    - President elected by plurality
    - *BUT* must also win at least 25% of the vote in 2/3 of Nigeria's states
        - 36 states, must win 25% in 24 states
        - Guarantees geographic representation
        - Federalism!

- Russia & Iran
    - Must win absolute majority in 1st or 2nd round
    - Allows for a *mandate to rule* (Authority granted to a government or leader to govern, typically  derived from the conset of the governed through elections or other legitimizing proesses)
        - Support of the people means they have authority to get stuff done


## Electoral Systems & Political Regimes
- Now, we will explore how political systems use *electoral regulatory organizations*
    - -> Regulatory groups that set various rules about ballot access and competition
    - Compare:
        - UK vs Iran vs Russia
            - UK -> Checked by the Oppositon party
            - Iran -> Guardian Council vets candidates to Majles
            - Russia -> Consitutionally the threshold rules of 5%


## Legislative Regulatory Commissions
- *UK*
    - Open ballot access for MPs
    - But the *House of Lords* has strict regulations
        - Appointments must be:
            1. Recommended by an independent commission to the PM
            2. Nominated by the PM
            3. Approved by the monarch
        - Encourages more diversity to replace hereditary appointments

- *Iran* 
    - *Guardian Council*
        - 6 members selected by the Supreme Leader
        - +6 from the judicary with the approval of the Majles
    - Allows for more diversity of members
        - But still... Supreme Leader has final say

- *Russia*
    - Appointments to the *Federation Council*
        - By regional governors and legislatures
        - 9 federal districts (including annexed Crimea)
            - Reasserts federal power under Putin by allowing him to:
                1. Appoint presidential envoys to each district
                2. Allowing regional legislatures to forego elections
        - Allows the regime to control the political agenda


## Presidential Election Regulations
1. Mexico -> plurality of the national popular vote, not an absolute majority
    - Federal Elections Institute (FEI) oversees voter registration, ballot design, vote counting, and disputes

2. Nigeria -> plurality, but candidates must secure 25% of the vote in 2/3 of the states
    - Reflects federalism
    - Independent Naitonal Electoral Commission (INEC) regulates elections
        - Began using biometric data in 2015
            - For registration and voting
            - Electronic voter cards
            - Improves credibility of elections

3. Russian & Iran -> absolute majority in 1st or 2nd round
    - Needs > 50% to win
    - No clear winner in the 1st round?
        - *Runoff elections* -> 2nd round with top 2 vote getters


## Importance of Term Limits
- Historical events and political shifts influence public priorities, and elections enable adaptability in leadership
- Term limits promote:
    1. Flexibility in governance
    2. Greater representation
    3. Diversity in political leadership
