# Nature and Role of Political Participation

## Learning Objectives
1. Explain the nature and role of political participation as related to a regime's use of authority and power

## Political Participation
- -> the way that individuals and groups engage in the political process
    1. Voluntary or coerced
    2. Indivdual or group basis

    - Crucial part of democratic governance
        - People allowed to be part of the desicion-making process
    - Correlated with strong civil society

- Forms of Participation:
    1. Voting (Formal)
        - Can be voluntary or coerced
            - Required in Australia and North Korea - not dependent on regime type
        - Ex. Mexico has compulsory voting but there are no penalties for not voting (?!?)

    2. Protests (Informal) -> in the form of groups or an indivudal
        - Happens in authoritarian and democratic regimes
            - Authoritarians encourage counter-protests that support the government
                - Used in both Russia and Iran


## Nature of Protest as a Means of Participation
- Protests can be and are often peaceful
    - Democratic societies usually require premits to protest

- Can also be violent
    - State (i.e. police) vs protesters
    - Counter-protesters vs protesters

- Many protests opposed the government
    - "We don't like ___ policy it's bad"
- Some protests support the government
    - Often done when one institution opposed another within
        - Patriotism/National Unity
        - Endorsement of policies
        - Mobilization against threats to government stability
    - State-led counter-protests often are part of this country

## Political Participation, Legitimacy, & Safety Values
- More participation => legitimacy (-> the right to rule as determined by citizens)
    - All regime types want legitimacy
    - Example: voting in Mexico, Nigeria, Russia

- Safety Values
    - -> processes that allow disconnected citizens to express themselves in order to prevent rebellion or revolution   
    - Protests, civil society, and referendums allow for safety values to prevent all-out rebellion

- Key Comparison:
    - *Authoritarian regimes are more likely to use citizen participation to intimidate opposition or give an illusion of influence, while democratic regimes hold elections to allow citizen control of the policy-making process*


## A Note About Referendums (referenda)
- -> allows citizens to vote directly on policy questions
    - Examples of *direct democracy*

- Used to:
    1. Promote democracy
    2. Allow the executive to bypass the legislature
    3. Oblige citizens to make diffcult and potentially unpopular desicions on public policy issues

- Example: UK
    - Decide questions about the devolution of powers to regional assemblies
    - Also used for Brexit
